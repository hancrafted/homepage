# Foundations

One durable principle, shown working, ending in something the viewer can try. 10–20 minutes. A takeaway is required, and the Publish bar applies in full.

Durable material is pre-hype canon — separation of concerns, design by contract, decomposition — translated for an audience that is not engineering. Showing today's agentic practice as an old idea wearing new clothes is what a sceptical audience needs to hear, and it is what gives the Episode shelf life.

## Beats

```markdown
## The practice they already run

## The model they are running

## The question that breaks it

## The explanation

## Shown working

## Where the analogy breaks down

## What to do
```

**The practice they already run.** Open inside something the audience already inhabits, drawn from a domain the user has worked in. The analogy is the entry point, not a decoration laid over a simplified version of the technical thing. Exempt from citation.

**The model they are running.** Name the outdated mental model out loud. The audience uses agentic tools daily; the gap is a stale model, not missing knowledge — so this beat surfaces the wrong model rather than explaining around it. Cite it: this is a claim about what people believe.

**The question that breaks it.** One question the stale model answers wrongly. This is the beat that makes the explanation land, and skipping it turns the Episode into a clear lecture that leaves the misconception intact while raising confidence.

**The explanation.** The durable principle, named the way the audience will recognise, then connected to what they are already doing with agentic tools. Cite it, and resolve the misconception explicitly — raising a wrong model without closing it leaves an audience with more misconceptions than saying nothing would have.

**Shown working.** A worked example, simple before complex. Cite it.

**Where the analogy breaks down.** State the limit: where the analogy stops mapping, and why the exception is deliberate. This is the most-skipped step in analogy-led teaching and skipping it is what produces analogy-induced misconceptions. A rule stated without its limit becomes a slogan and then gets ignored.

**What to do.** Reach back to the analogy to motivate it — _you wouldn't do this in the analogy, so what would you do here?_ Exempt from citation.

Return to the analogy at each major turn, not only in the opening. One analogy carried the length of an Episode is worth more than three good ones used once each.
